About

Associate Project Manager at Intuji

I manage client software projects end to end: scoping and planning, cross-team execution, dependency tracking, surfacing risk early, and the handover at the finish. Most of my current work is NetSuite implementations and managed services for Australian clients, plus the tenders that win the next project.

I work closely with engineering and product on production-impacting changes, and I care about system behaviour, root causes and downstream impact rather than the ticket in front of me. That is what lets a team decide quickly without losing ownership as work crosses functions.

It comes from years in customer experience and technical support, owning complex production issues from intake to resolution. It still shapes how I run projects: I write updates people can act on, and I hold expectations steady when a date starts to move.

My strength is technical fluency and context building, not hands-on development.

How I work

Six things I hold to, whatever the project is

The update goes out before you ask for it

Nobody should have to chase a status. Updates go out on a rhythm, structured and accurate, so that quiet weeks are information too rather than a gap you have to interpret.

Risk gets named early, even when it is awkward

A slipping dependency is only a crisis if nobody said so in week two. I would rather have the uncomfortable conversation while there are still options on the table.

Cause before symptom

I look at system behaviour and downstream impact rather than the ticket in front of me. It costs a day at the start and saves a fortnight by the middle.

Handover is part of delivery

If support inherits a build nobody documented, the project is not finished, it has just stopped. Written handover is a deliverable, not an afterthought.

Written first, because the team is never all awake

With delivery in Kathmandu and clients further east and west, anything that exists only inside a call does not really exist. Decisions get written down where the next person will find them.

Scope narrowly and be right

An estimate that survives contact with month two is worth more than a generous one you have to renegotiate. I would rather commit to less and deliver all of it.
